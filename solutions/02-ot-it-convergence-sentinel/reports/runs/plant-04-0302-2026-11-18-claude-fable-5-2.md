## The planner's reasoning

> Recommendation for the Head of OT Security: disconnect Line B, cell 2 AND close the link between office side and plant floor. Both together, not one of them.
> Where the intruder is: on the remote access gateway and on the engineering workstation in Line B, cell 2. From that workstation the programs of both cell 2 controllers were copied (02:52, 02:55), and at 03:02 it searched for file shares on the operator panel of Line C, cell 1. There is an old file share between Line B cell 2 and Line C cell 1, left open after a past project.
> The five options, priced:
> 1. Do nothing: costs EUR 0 now. Lines A, B and C stay within reach. EUR 2160000 at stake.
> 2. Close the office-plant link only: costs EUR 0 (lines run on loaded orders). Line A is out of reach, but Lines B and C stay within reach from the engineering workstation in cell 2 and the old file share. EUR 1440000 still at stake.
> 3. Disconnect Line B cell 2 only: costs EUR 120000 (Line B stopped 8 hours). Lines A, B and C all stay within reach, because the remote access gateway is still under the intruder's control and still leads to the floor. EUR 2160000 still at stake. This is paying for a stop and gaining nothing.
> 4. Both steps: costs EUR 120000 (Line B stopped 8 hours; A keeps running, C is in planned maintenance until 08:00). No line left within reach from the known footholds. EUR 0 still at stake.
> 5. Disconnect the whole plant floor: costs EUR 285500 (Lines A and B 8 hours each, Line C about 3 hours after its maintenance ends). No line left within reach. EUR 0 still at stake.
> Option 4 closes the same known paths as option 5 for EUR 120000 instead of EUR 285500. It is the narrowest stop that leaves no line within reach of what we know about. The office-side steps already taken (two accounts suspended, finance workstation quarantined, remote session closed) do not remove the intruder from the engineering workstation in cell 2, so they are not enough on their own.
> Safety first: before cell 2 is disconnected, the Line B shift lead must be told so the line is brought to a stop in a controlled way, and the maintenance crew on Line C must be told that their operator panel was probed at 03:02.

What remains open, in the planner's words:

> Option 4 closes every path we know of. It is not proof the plant is clean. Open points:
> - We do not know whether the two controller programs in Line B cell 2 were only copied or also changed. Line B must not restart until they are checked against the last approved versions.
> - We do not know whether the intruder already got onto anything in Line C cell 1. We saw a search for file shares on the operator panel, nothing more. Line C stays connected to the rest of the floor under option 4. If the panel or controller of Line C is already under the intruder's control, only option 5 (EUR 285500) covers that. Line C is in maintenance until 08:00, which gives time to check it before it starts.
> - We do not know whether there are footholds we have not seen. All prices assume the three known ones (finance workstation, remote access gateway, engineering workstation in cell 2).
> - We do not know what the intruder wants. The amounts at stake assume the worst case of encrypted lines with 48 hours to restore; this is an assumption, not an observation.
> - The EUR 0 for closing the link rests on two assumptions: lines run 6 hours on loaded orders, and the gateway is clean in 4 hours. If cleaning takes longer than 6 hours, Lines A and C run out of orders and stop; that cost is not priced here.
> - The 8 hours for Line B is an assumption for checking and cleaning one cell. EUR 15,000 per stopped line-hour is an assumption from the company profile.
> - The copied controller programs are in the intruder's hands. No step tonight takes that back.
> - The old file share between Line B cell 2 and Line C cell 1 stays in place; it is only cut off while cell 2 is disconnected. It must be removed before cell 2 is reconnected.
> - With the link closed there is no remote access to the floor and the production data recorder gets no data from the floor for those hours.
> The decision is the Head of OT Security's. Nothing on the plant floor has been disconnected.

> The recommendation stands. The decider should add three conditions the planner did not state; they are in the unknowns below. None of them changes which option to pick tonight. They change when it is safe to reopen the link and reconnect.

## Assumptions behind the figures

- Cost of one stopped line-hour: EUR 15,000 (company profile).
- Assumption: a line keeps running this long on orders already loaded when the link to the office side is closed. Value used: 6 hours.
- Assumption: time to restore a line whose controllers and workstations were encrypted. Value used: 48 hours.
- Assumption: a disconnected cell stops its whole line. Value used: yes.
- Disconnect Line B, cell 2: held for 8 hours. Assumption: time to check and clean the devices of one cell.
- Close the link between office side and plant floor: held for 4 hours. Assumption: time to clean the remote access gateway and replace the stolen account.
- Disconnect the whole plant floor: held for 8 hours. Assumption: time to check and clean the devices of the plant floor.
- Lost output is counted only for hours in which the schedule says the line would have run.
- The office-side steps do not change the prices: a device the intruder already controls on the plant floor is assumed to stay under its control.
